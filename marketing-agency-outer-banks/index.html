Straight answers

What should you look for in a marketing agency in the Outer Banks?

Extra Medium is a marketing agency in the Outer Banks, built for businesses that live and die by the season. We run the full mix — strategy, branding, web, SEO, paid media, social, creators and email — aimed at one job: fuller calendars in summer, and a real business the rest of the year.

A market with one long summer and one long winter

The Outer Banks does not run on a normal business calendar. From Memorial Day to Labor Day, the islands from Corolla to Hatteras run at full capacity: rental houses full, restaurants on a wait, charters booked out. Then the crowds go home, and a long quiet season tests every business that summer carried. Marketing here has one job in two acts. Win the booking season, when families choose houses, weddings and weeks months ahead of arrival. Then build the shoulder seasons and the off-season, so the business is not starting from zero every spring. An agency that treats the Outer Banks like an ordinary market will spend your money in the wrong months, aimed at the wrong people.

The full mix, under one roof

Strategy comes first: who the business is for, what a good season actually looks like, and which months have to carry it. Under that sit the channels, each with a local job to do. Branding and creative that look like the islands, not a stock beach. Web design built for booking on a phone. SEO for the town-by-town searches travelers actually make. Paid search and paid social aimed at drive-distance cities, from Richmond and Hampton Roads to Raleigh and DC. Social media and influencer and creator marketing that shows real weeks in real houses. Content and email that bring last year’s guests back before the platforms sell them to someone else. Our services page sets out the studio in full.

Senior people, plain talk, seasonal sense

Most Outer Banks businesses are small and family-run, and the owner is also the bookkeeper, the maintenance call and the complaint department. We work accordingly: senior people only, a written plan shaped around your season, and reporting against bookings and revenue instead of likes. You will always know what is running, what it cost and what it produced. A look at the work shows the standard we hold for our own products as well as client work, because the advice we sell is the advice we run on.

Key takeaways

  • On the Outer Banks, marketing has one job in two acts: win the booking season, then build the rest of the year.
  • The businesses that win the summer start marketing in the winter, when everyone else has gone dark.
  • Judge an Outer Banks agency on bookings and revenue, not on likes and reach.

Asked often.

Do you only work with tourism businesses on the Outer Banks?

No. Tourism drives the economy, but the islands also run on the businesses that serve the people who live here year-round: home services, healthcare, real estate and professional firms. The seasonal discipline applies to nearly everyone here, because even a local practice feels the summer swell and the winter quiet.

When should Outer Banks marketing actually run?

Earlier than feels natural. Families book summer houses in the winter, destination weddings book a year or more out, and search rankings take months to build. The businesses that win the season start their marketing in the quiet months, while everyone else has gone dark.

How do we start?

In writing. Send a note through our contact page saying what you run and what a good season looks like. You will get a specific reply: whether we can help, what we would do first, and what it would cost, before any commitment.
