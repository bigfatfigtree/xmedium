The problem

Influencer marketing skipped local.

A neighborhood restaurant doesn’t need a creator with two million followers in another state. It needs the 2,000 people within walking distance who decide where Friday night happens — and those people follow neighbors, not influencers.

Meanwhile local creators with small, fiercely local audiences had no front door: brands couldn’t find them, couldn’t book them properly, and couldn’t tell what a visit brought in.

So we built the front door — a two-sided platform where the standards are the marketing: published pricing, a two-business-day decision on every application, tracked visits, and a Visit Guarantee.

Built by the studio

Brand. Product. Platform. Pricing. Go-to-market. Ops.

What we built

Everything a launch takes. Ours.

Brand & naming — Magpie, a one-word name with its gradient identity system, designed here.

Product & platform — creator applications with automatic tiering (Neighbors 500–2k, Locals 2k–15k, Headliners 15k+, stacked across platforms), business onboarding, and the offer → visit → post → tracked-outcome lifecycle.

Business model — two lanes: comped visits, and paid bookings where creators keep 97%. Plans and pricing are published on the product site — in the open, like everything else.

Go-to-market — city-by-city founding rosters, hand-picked market by market, home market Virginia Beach first. Operations — run daily by this studio, under Extra Medium LLC.
