Straight answers

What are the most common small business marketing mistakes?

The same five, on repeat: boosting posts instead of running a strategy, copying what big brands do, leading with no real offer, never following up on the interest marketing creates, and only marketing when sales dip. None of them is a budget problem. All of them are fixable in the order they are listed.

Activity dressed up as strategy

Boosting a post feels like marketing: money goes out, numbers come back. But a boost just pays to distribute a post that never had a job, to an audience chosen by a button. Underneath it there is no decision about who this is for or what they should do next. The companion mistake is copying big brands. National companies run brand campaigns because they already have distribution, shelf space and budgets that buy reach by the ton. A local business copying the style without the scale gets the expense of brand advertising with none of the machinery that makes it pay.

No offer, and no follow-up

Most small business marketing points at nothing: no specific offer, no reason to act now, just a general hope that people remember the name. Then, when the marketing does work and someone raises a hand, the follow-up leaks it away — the inquiry answered tomorrow, the estimate sent next week, the lead left in an inbox. Interest decays in hours, not weeks. These two mistakes together mean businesses pay to create demand and then decline to collect it, which is why they are more expensive than any line in the ad budget.

Marketing only when sales dip

The feast-or-famine cycle: sales slow down, panic marketing starts, work arrives, marketing stops because everyone is busy, the pipeline quietly empties, sales slow down. Marketing has a lag — the customers you need in six weeks are decided by the presence you build now — so switching it on during the famine guarantees the famine lasts. A steady, modest presence beats heroic bursts every time. If your results feel random, the longer diagnosis in why your marketing is not working will sound familiar, and the studio’s Grow practice is built around exactly this rhythm problem.

Key takeaways

  • Boosting a post is paying to distribute a decision you never made.
  • Interest decays in hours. Follow-up is marketing, not admin.
  • Marketing only when sales dip guarantees the dip lasts.

Asked often.

We have almost no budget. What matters most?

The cheapest fixes are the biggest ones: a concrete offer, same-day follow-up, and being easy to find locally cost little or nothing. Fix the leaks before buying more reach to pour into them.

Which mistake should we fix first?

Follow-up. It is the only fix that makes every dollar you already spend work harder, starting today, with no new budget and no new tools.
