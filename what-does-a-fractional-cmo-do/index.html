Straight answers

What does a fractional CMO actually do all week?

A fractional CMO owns your marketing the way a full-time CMO would, for a set share of the week. They set the strategy and positioning, decide the priorities and the budget, lead whoever is doing the work, and answer for the results. The difference from a full-time hire is the calendar, not the seniority: same seat, fewer days. Most engagements run one to three days a week, inside your leadership meetings and your numbers.

Strategy and positioning first

The week starts with decisions, not tasks. A fractional CMO settles who the customer is, what the company promises them, and which few channels deserve the budget. Everything else gets cut or parked. That work turns into a written plan with 90-day priorities, so the team is executing against something instead of improvising. Positioning, pricing input, and the message the whole company uses all sit in this seat. If your company is still deciding whether it needs this at all, the signs are laid out in when to hire a fractional CMO.

Leading the people doing the work

Most growing companies already have hands: a junior marketer or two, an agency, a few freelancers. What they do not have is anyone senior telling those hands what matters. The fractional CMO briefs the agency, reviews the work before it ships, kills the projects that do not serve the plan, and coaches the internal team. Hiring sits here too: writing the marketing job descriptions, interviewing, and deciding who to bring in next. The seat also manages vendors and budgets, so spend stops drifting across tools and retainers nobody is watching.

Reporting against revenue, and building what stays

The seat sits in your leadership meetings and reports the way an executive does: pipeline, revenue, cost per customer, and what changes next because of them. Not impressions, not activity. A good fractional CMO also builds the systems that outlast the engagement: the dashboard, the planning rhythm, the brief templates, the scorecard the team keeps using. When the engagement ends, the function keeps running. What this cadence costs is covered in what a fractional CMO costs; at Extra Medium the seat comes with the studio underneath it, so the strategy and the execution live in one company.

Asked often.

How many hours a week does a fractional CMO work?

Published guides put most engagements at one to three days a week, roughly 8 to 24 hours. The right cadence depends on team size and how much is moving: a launch quarter needs more than a steady-state quarter. The cadence is agreed up front and can flex as the work does.

Does a fractional CMO do the work themselves or direct it?

Both, in different proportions. The seat directs: strategy, priorities, briefs, reviews, and the numbers. Hands-on execution (design, copy, media buying, development) usually sits with your team, your agencies, or, at Extra Medium, the studio under the same roof. Expect the CMO to write the plan and the brief, not the social posts.

How fast should we see results?

Published guides put time to value at roughly 30 to 45 days: the audit, the plan, and the first corrections land in the first month or two. Revenue movement takes longer and depends on your sales cycle. Anyone promising a specific return in a specific week is guessing, and you should price that guess accordingly.
