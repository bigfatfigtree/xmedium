Straight answers

What does a digital marketing agency in Charlottesville do differently?

Extra Medium is a marketing agency in Charlottesville with a digital practice built for a market that runs on seasons: UVA’s calendar, harvest in the Monticello AVA, and a steady flow of visitors. We run SEO, paid search, paid social, email and web as one system, timed to when this city actually buys.

One system, not five vendors

The usual small-market setup is five freelancers and a cousin: one for the website, one for ads, one for social, none of them talking. The reports never add up and the customer journey falls through the seams. We run SEO, paid search, paid social, email, content and web as one system with one report, as described in our services practice — so the ad, the page it lands on and the email that follows tell the same story, and the budget follows whichever part of the system is producing bookings, sales or sign-ups.

Timed to Charlottesville’s calendar

Demand here is not flat, and flat spending wastes it. Visitors research wine country weeks before they arrive; students and parents surge around move-in and graduation; locals book dinners, appointments and services on a weekly rhythm that shifts with the university’s breaks. Digital budgets should breathe with that calendar — heavier ahead of harvest and football weekends, smarter in the quiet weeks, always built on the search terms people use when the trip is still being planned, not after they arrive.

Email, and the customer who comes back

Visitors buy once. Locals and alumni buy for years — if you stay in touch. Email is the unglamorous channel that carries that relationship: the winery’s release announcement, the restaurant’s slow-Tuesday offer, the shop’s note to alumni on a home-game weekend. We build lists honestly, write emails worth opening, and count repeat revenue as the metric it is. If your digital marketing needs a system instead of a pile of accounts, start a conversation and we will map the first move in writing.

Key takeaways

  • Charlottesville buys on a calendar. Market to the calendar, not the clock.
  • Five vendors, five reports, no system. One team fixes that.
  • Visitors book once. Locals and alumni come back — email is how you keep them.

Asked often.

Should Charlottesville businesses advertise all year or seasonally?

Both, weighted. A steady base keeps you findable for locals year-round; the spend should lean into the windows when this market decides — trip-planning weeks before harvest and football weekends, move-in, graduation, and the holiday season. Flat budgets in a seasonal market pay peak prices for off-peak attention.

Is paid search worth it in a small market like Charlottesville?

Often, yes, precisely because it is small: the auctions are less crowded than a metro’s, and the searches are high-intent — someone looking for a tasting room, a hotel or a dinner reservation this weekend is close to buying. It fails when the landing page wastes the click, which is a fixable problem.

How do we start?

In writing. Send the problem through our contact page and you will get a specific reply: whether we can help, what we would do first, and what it would cost. Nobody books a call on your calendar unless you ask for one.
