Straight answers

Why is my marketing not working?

Marketing usually fails for one of five reasons: there is no strategy underneath it, it is aimed at the wrong audience, the offer is weak, the follow-through stops early, or success is being measured in likes instead of revenue. The fix starts with diagnosis, not more activity — more of the wrong thing is just more.

The five usual suspects

No strategy. Channels are running, posts are going out, ads are live — and nobody can say what it is all for, in one sentence. Activity without a decision underneath it is just noise with a budget. Wrong audience. The work is aimed at everyone, or at people who admire the product but cannot buy it. A weak offer. There is nothing concrete to say yes to, so interest has nowhere to go. Follow-through. Leads get answered late or never, and campaigns get killed at week three, right before compounding starts. Measurement. The dashboard celebrates reach and likes while revenue sits in a different report nobody opens.

How to tell which one is yours

Run five quick tests. Can you state the strategy in one sentence without naming a channel? If not, that is suspect one. Compare who engages with your marketing to who actually buys: if they are different people, that is suspect two. Read your offer as a stranger: is there a specific thing to accept, with a reason to accept it now? Mystery-shop your own lead flow and time the response: anything slower than the same business day is suspect four at work. Finally, look at the number your team checks every Monday. If it is not connected to revenue, suspect five is running the meeting.

Fix the diagnosis before the budget

The instinct when marketing fails is to spend more or switch channels. Both moves skip the only step that matters: finding out which of the five is broken. The repair order is the order above — strategy first, then audience, offer, follow-through, measurement — because each one makes the next one cheaper. This is the diagnosis-first sequence behind how the studio works, and it is also the core of the fractional CMO seat: a senior operator whose first job is to find the real constraint, not to sell you more activity.

Key takeaways

  • More of the wrong thing is just more.
  • Marketing rarely fails in the channels. It fails in the aiming.
  • Diagnose before you spend. The order of the fix is the order of the problem.

Asked often.

How long should we give marketing before judging it?

Long enough to measure a real cycle. Most programs need a full quarter of consistent execution against one strategy before the verdict means anything. Judging at week three measures the launch, not the marketing.

Should we fire our agency and start over?

Sometimes. But if the strategy was never set, the next agency inherits the same problem with a new logo on the reports. Diagnose first; the answer is often direction, not personnel.

How do we start with Extra Medium?
