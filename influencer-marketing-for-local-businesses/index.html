Straight answers

Does influencer marketing work for local businesses?

Yes, when the influencer is actually local. A creator whose followers live in your trade area can fill tables, book appointments and move product in a way a celebrity post never will, because their audience can act on it today. The discipline is checking local reach before you book — not follower count, which measures the wrong map.

Why local beats famous for a local business

Every audience has a geography. A celebrity’s million followers are spread across every time zone, which means almost none of them can book your table, your chair or your service this week. A local creator’s audience is concentrated where your customers live, and it comes with a second advantage money cannot buy at national scale: trust. Their followers know their streets, their tastes and their honesty, because the creator’s life looks like theirs. For a business that sells within a few miles, a smaller audience that can actually walk in beats a famous one that can only watch.

Comped experience or paid post?

The two standard arrangements buy different things. A comped experience trades the product itself — the meal, the service, the visit — for the creator’s coverage of it. It suits restaurants, salons, attractions and services where the experience is the story, and it keeps the post honest, because the creator genuinely had it. A paid post buys defined deliverables at the creator’s rate: specific posts, specific timing, a launch or campaign that needs control. Either way, agree the deliverables, the timing and how the content may be reused, in writing, before anything is posted. Handshakes are how good intentions turn into mismatched expectations.

Check the map before you book

Follower count measures the wrong map. Before booking anyone, look at where their audience actually is: ask for the geography behind the account, read the comments for local names and local references, and weigh engagement from your area over raw totals. This discipline is why our lab built Magpie, a local creator platform that shows each creator’s local reach — the share of their audience that actually lives in your market — before anyone books, so a business can choose creators by the audience that can act, not the biggest number on the profile. Magpie is one of the products in development and live in Extra Medium Labs, where the studio tests its own advice with its own money first.

Key takeaways

  • Follower count measures the wrong map. Local reach is the number that spends.
  • A local audience can act today. A famous one can only watch.
  • Comp the experience when the experience is the story. Pay when you need control.

Asked often.

How much do local creators charge?

It varies by creator and by what is being asked. At the local level, many creators happily work for a comped experience; established local voices list a rate for paid posts with defined deliverables. The right comparison is cost against the customers their local audience can actually send you.

How do we find creators in our area?

Start with who already posts about your category in your town, then check the geography of their audience before their follower count. Platforms like Magpie exist to make that check the first step instead of an afterthought.
