Fractional CMO in Chicago: what does it cost, and who does it fit?

In Chicago, a fractional CMO gives a growing company senior marketing leadership part-time, usually one to three days a week, for a monthly retainer instead of a full-time executive package. Published Chicago pricing commonly runs $5,000 to $20,000 a month. Extra Medium works with Chicago companies the same way: founder-led, senior-only leadership that owns the plan, the budget and the team’s direction, run remotely day to day and in the room when it matters. The exact price is in writing before work begins.

A B2B town that runs on payback math

Chicago is a B2B town. Manufacturing, logistics, food, distribution and professional services anchor the economy, with a large startup scene layered on top. Marketing buyers here are practical: they expect plain payback math, cost per qualified opportunity and revenue per channel, not impressions and reach. That makes the fractional model a natural fit. Most of these companies have real marketing activity and real spend, but the plan lives in pieces: an agency here, a freelancer there, a sales team working from different assumptions. A fractional CMO joins it up, sets priorities against the numbers, and is accountable for what the spend returns.

What it costs in Chicago

Published 2026 pricing for Chicago puts fractional CMO retainers commonly between $5,000 and $20,000 a month, depending on days per week and scope. Seasoned operators billing hourly run roughly $200 to $350 an hour. Chicago rates sit about 15 to 20 percent below New York and San Francisco for comparable seniority, which is part of the city’s appeal: the leadership is the same caliber, the overhead is not. Extra Medium does not publish a rate card. Your engagement is priced on the days and the remit, and you get the exact figure in writing before any work begins.

Who it fits, and how it starts

It fits the Chicago pattern well: a manufacturer or distributor with a strong product and marketing that has not kept up, a services firm whose growth still runs on referrals, or a startup past its first traction that needs a plan and a leader before it needs a bigger team. It does not fit if the need is hands alone, or a leader on site five days a week. Working together runs on a weekly cadence, remotely day to day and in the room for planning, launches and board-level moments. It starts with a free conversation, followed by the engagement shape and price in writing.
