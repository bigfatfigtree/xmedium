Straight answers

Fractional CMO or marketing agency: which does your company need?

An agency executes: give it a brief and it produces campaigns, creative and media. A fractional CMO leads: they own the strategy, write the brief, manage whoever executes, and answer for the outcome. If your problem is that work isn’t getting made, you need an agency. If your problem is that nobody senior can say what work should be made, or why, you need the CMO seat filled first — otherwise the agency will execute the wrong thing beautifully.

The difference, plainly

Agencies are production capacity plus channel expertise, hired by the project or the month. They are excellent at making things and are structurally rewarded for making more things. A fractional CMO is leadership capacity: diagnosis, strategy, prioritization, vendor management and accountability in the leadership meeting. They are rewarded for outcomes, including the outcome of doing less, better.

When each one is the right buy

Buy the agency when the strategy is settled and the constraint is hands: a launch date, a content engine, a media plan that needs running. Buy the fractional CMO when the strategy is the constraint: spend spread across channels nobody can connect to revenue, agencies unmanaged, a founder making every marketing decision by default. Buy both, in sequence, when you need the strategy set and then executed without a hand-off — which is most growing companies, most of the time.

Why Extra Medium is both

The studio was built so the two never sit in different companies arguing over whose fault the quarter was. The Lead practice sets the strategy; the Launch, Reinvent and Grow practices execute it; and Labs keeps both honest by spending the studio’s own money on the same advice. One roof, one accountable team.

Asked often.

Can a fractional CMO manage our existing agency?

Yes — that is one of the highest-value uses of the seat. A senior operator on your side of the table briefs the agency properly, judges the work against strategy, and stops spend that can’t defend itself.

We already have a marketing manager. Do we still need a fractional CMO?

Often, yes, and it’s the combination that works best: the manager runs the week, the fractional CMO sets the direction, develops the manager, and carries the senior conversations. It’s how many companies grow into a full-time seat.

What if we choose wrong?

Start with diagnosis. A short strategy phase shows whether your constraint is direction or production before you commit to either shape. That is how Extra Medium starts most engagements.
