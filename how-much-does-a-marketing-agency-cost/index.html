Straight answers

How much does a marketing agency cost?

A marketing agency typically charges a monthly retainer, and the honest answer is that the range is wide because the work is: a narrow, well-defined scope costs far less than a full growth function. What you are really buying is seniority, scope and speed — and the cheapest quote is usually the most expensive mistake.

What actually drives the number

Four things set the price. Scope: one channel run well costs a fraction of a full function — strategy, creative, media, content and reporting under one roof. Seniority: you are paying for the judgment of the people on your account, and a senior operator costs more per hour than a junior with a checklist. Speed: launches and turnarounds compress timelines, and compressed timelines cost. Strategy: execution-only engagements look cheaper until you realize nobody is deciding what the execution is for. One more thing that is not in the fee: ad spend. The retainer buys the people and the thinking; the media budget is yours and goes to the platforms.

What cheap really costs

A cheap quote usually buys junior hours, template work and activity that was never aimed at anything. The invoice is small; the damage is not. It shows up as a year of media spend pointed at the wrong audience, creative that has to be redone by the next firm, and a leadership team that concludes “marketing doesn’t work” when what actually failed was the aiming. The most expensive line in most marketing budgets is never the agency fee. It is the spend that ran all year underneath a strategy nobody set.

Retainer, project, or a seat at the table

The three shapes buy different things. A project buys a defined outcome — a launch, a rebrand, a website. A retainer buys an ongoing function: the calendar run, the channels managed, the reporting rhythm kept. Fractional leadership buys direction itself, for companies whose real gap is that nobody senior is setting the strategy the other two shapes execute. Published market ranges give the shape of it: fractional CMO retainers typically run $3,000–$20,000 a month, while a full-time CMO typically costs $300K–$570K a year fully loaded — which is exactly why the fractional seat exists. Our breakdown of what a fractional CMO costs goes deeper, and the fractional CMO practice explains how the seat works.

Key takeaways

  • You are not buying hours. You are buying seniority, scope and speed.
  • The cheapest quote is usually the most expensive mistake.
  • The agency fee is rarely the waste. The unaimed spend underneath it is.

Asked often.

Is ad spend included in the agency fee?

Almost never. The retainer pays for strategy and the people who plan, create and manage the work; the media budget is separate and goes to the platforms. Any quote that blurs the two into one number deserves a second look.

Why do quotes for the same work vary so much?

Because the work is not the same. Quotes differ on who actually does it, whether strategy is included, how much reporting and senior attention you get, and how much of the team is senior versus handed down. The deliverables list can look identical while the judgment behind it is not.
