Straight answers

Are paid ads worth it for a small business?

Paid ads are worth it when three things are true: the margin on a new customer covers the cost of buying one, people are already searching for what you sell, and someone follows up fast when interest arrives. Miss any one of the three and ads do not fail loudly — they just quietly burn the budget.

Run the math before you spend

Three numbers decide whether ads can work for you. The margin on a new customer: what one sale actually contributes after the costs of delivering it. The value over time: whether that customer comes back, refers others, or is gone forever. And your close rate: of the people who raise a hand, how many become customers. The arithmetic is simple. If a new customer is worth $400 in margin and you close one lead in four, a lead can cost up to $100 before the math breaks — and you would rather it cost half that. If your real numbers make a lead worth $12, no platform, targeting trick or agency can save the campaign. The math is the strategy.

When paid works, and when it burns

Paid works when it intercepts existing demand: people already searching for the thing, an offer worth clicking, a page that continues the promise the ad made, and follow-up measured in minutes. Paid burns when it is asked to manufacture demand for a weak offer, when margins are too thin to buy attention at auction prices, when traffic lands on a homepage that says nothing specific, or when leads sit unanswered until the interest is cold. Notice that none of the failure modes live inside the ad account. The platform gets blamed for arithmetic and follow-up that were broken before the first dollar was spent.

Sequence: earn it, then amplify it

The order matters. First, fix the offer and the follow-up, because ads multiply whatever is already true about your business — including the leaks. Second, build the free foundation: the local presence and search visibility in how local businesses get more customers, which keeps producing after the budget pauses. Third, use paid to pour volume into what already converts, and to test offers faster than organic reach allows. Paid and organic are not rivals; paid is the accelerator, not the engine. The studio’s Grow practice runs them in that order for exactly this reason.

Key takeaways

  • Ads multiply whatever is already true about your business, including the leaks.
  • The math is the strategy: margin, lifetime value, close rate.
  • Fix the offer and the follow-up first. Then buy volume.

Asked often.

How much should a small business spend on ads?

There is no standard number, and anyone who gives you one before asking about your margins is guessing with your money. Start with a test budget sized by the math: enough to buy real data on your cost per lead, small enough that the lesson is affordable either way.

Should we start with search ads or social ads?

If people are actively searching for what you sell, search captures that demand first. Social interrupts rather than answers, so it suits visual offers, local experiences and creating demand that does not exist yet. The right order follows where the intent already lives.
