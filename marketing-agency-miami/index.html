Marketing in a city the world visits

Most cities market to the people who live there. Miami markets to two audiences at once: the visitors who arrive all year for the beaches, the cruise port, the art fairs and the events calendar, and the locals who stay after the visitors leave. Those audiences want different things, search at different times and book on different clocks. A hotel, restaurant group or attraction that runs one message for both usually ends up half right for each. We build Miami marketing as the two-front job it actually is, with campaigns planned around the visitor economy and the local one, and measured on bookings and revenue rather than impressions.

One team, one strategy

The usual Miami setup is a patchwork: a social freelancer, a paid media buyer, a web firm from two redesigns ago, and strategy living in nobody’s head. Extra Medium runs the whole mix under one roof: strategy, digital, SEO, paid media, social, creators, content and creative, with fractional CMO leadership in Miami on top when the executive seat is the real gap. One team owns the plan and the budget, so the channels stop competing with each other and start compounding. What it costs is not a mystery either: the engagement shape and price are in writing before work begins, and our guide to what a marketing agency costs shows the math in the open.

Built for how Miami does business

Miami business runs on several engines at once: hospitality and tourism, Brickell finance, the Wynwood creative economy, and the Latin American companies that run their US operations from here. It is a bilingual market in practice, not in slogan: campaigns here are planned for English and Spanish audiences from the start, not translated at the end. The practice is founder-led and senior-only, led by an operator with two decades in local media and startups, and the studio behind it also builds: our Labs arm built Magpie, a local creator marketing platform, and Hapsta, a live platform at hapsta.co. The team that markets your company can also build what it launches on.

Key takeaways

  • Miami markets to two audiences at once: the world that visits and the locals who stay.
  • In a bilingual gateway city, campaigns planned in one language leave half the market unaddressed.
  • Hospitality, finance and events move fast here; layered agencies slow the response.

Asked often.

Do you work with hospitality and tourism businesses in Miami?

Yes. Hotels, restaurants, attractions and events are core Miami work. The discipline is treating visitors and locals as two audiences with different booking clocks, then building campaigns for each instead of one message averaged between them.

Can you run campaigns in English and Spanish?

Miami is a bilingual market, and campaigns here are planned for both audiences from the start where the business serves both. That means real strategy and copy for each audience, not the English campaign translated the night before launch.
