Straight answers

What does a fractional CMO in Nashville do, and when do you need one?

Extra Medium is a marketing agency in Nashville, and our fractional CMO practice puts a senior marketing executive inside your company part-time. Companies in healthcare, hospitality and the music business get leadership, a plan and a team being led, without a full-time executive package. The seat is senior-only, never handed off.

Why Nashville companies rent the seat

Nashville grows companies past founder-led marketing at unusual speed. Healthcare companies scale out of the hub’s orbit, hospitality groups add rooms and restaurants across a visitor economy that never really slows, music businesses professionalize, and a fast-growing population keeps minting new local brands. The pattern repeats: the spend is real, the activity is everywhere, and nobody senior owns the strategy. A fractional CMO fills exactly that gap, an executive who has run the function before, inside your business part-time, accountable in the leadership meeting.

What the seat actually does

The week is concrete. Positioning first: who the company is for, in a city where you might sell to tourists, newcomers and lifelong locals in the same day. Then the plan and the budget, with every vendor, freelancer and channel held to it. Then the team: our senior-only studio can execute behind the seat, or the seat can lead the people and agencies you already have. Behind the seat sit two things most fractional executives cannot offer: an AI practice that builds research, content and AI search visibility into the function, and a Labs arm that has built and launched its own platforms, Magpie for local creator marketing and Hapsta, live at hapsta.co. Two decades in local media and startups stand behind the judgment.

The math, honestly

Published market ranges put US fractional CMO retainers typically at $3,000 to $20,000 a month depending on cadence and scope, while a full-time CMO typically costs $300,000 to $570,000 a year once salary, bonus, benefits and equity are loaded in. Those are published ranges, not our price: every engagement here is shaped in the first conversation and put in writing before work begins. The fuller comparison lives on our fractional CMO cost page. The short version: you pay for leadership days, skip the executive package, and the seat usually earns its keep first by stopping spend nobody senior was questioning.

Key takeaways

  • Nashville growth outruns founder-led marketing; the missing piece is a senior seat, not more activity.
  • A fractional CMO owns positioning, plan and budget, and answers for them in the leadership meeting.
  • Published ranges put fractional leadership at $3,000 to $20,000 a month, against $300,000 to $570,000 a year loaded for a full-time CMO.

Asked often.

How many days a week does a fractional CMO work?

Most engagements run one to three days a week equivalent, weighted toward planning cycles, launches and leadership meetings. The shape is set against what actually needs leading: a hospitality group heading into peak season needs more seat time than a company holding a steady course.

Does a fractional CMO replace our agency or our marketing hire?

No. The seat leads them. Agencies and junior marketers execute; the CMO writes the brief, owns the strategy and judges the work. Many Nashville companies keep their existing team and finally give it senior direction. At Extra Medium the seat and the studio sit in one company, which removes the hand-off entirely if you want it to.

How do we start?

With a first conversation that is free and specific to your situation: whether the seat fits, what the cadence would look like, and what it would cost, in plain terms. If it fits, the engagement shape and price arrive in writing before any work begins.
